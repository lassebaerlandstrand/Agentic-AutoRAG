## How RAG optimization works

A RAG pipeline is two stages in sequence: retrieval (surface the gold spans a
question needs) then generation (answer from them). The metrics isolate the two,
which is what makes this search efficient — you can work on each stage and read
the result off the metric that owns it, instead of sweeping the whole grid:

- `retrieval_complete` — fraction of questions where every gold span was
  retrieved. A pure property of the RETRIEVAL stack.
  Levers: embedding_model, reranker, top_k, index_type, chunking_strategy,
  chunk_token_size, hybrid_alpha, query_expansion.
- `acc_given_complete` — of the questions whose context WAS complete, the
  fraction answered correctly. A pure property of the GENERATOR.
  Levers: generator_llm, reasoning.
- `retrieval_partial` with a low source-fact rank means the gold span was
  retrieved but ranked below the cutoff — a ranking problem.
  Levers: reranker, reranker_top_n.

A question is right only when retrieval surfaces the spans AND the generator uses
them, so the achievable score is gated by BOTH stages, and each metric attributes
a change to the stage that owns it, so you can tell a retrieval problem from a
generation one. The component bests in the state card are the best you have
OBSERVED so far, not the ceiling the corpus allows: a component that barely moves
however you push it may already be at that ceiling, and a low number is not
automatically the one worth fixing. The two stages are largely separable but not
perfectly — how much retrieved context a generator turns into correct answers
depends on the model, so a retrieval recipe that is best for one generator is not
guaranteed best for another. Treat the split as a strong guide, not a guarantee,
and verify with the metrics.

## Your campaign plan

Run this as a deliberate campaign, not trial-by-trial reflexes. You carry a plan
across trials and revise it as evidence lands. Each trial you are shown your own
plan from last trial (in the state card) — honor it or revise it on purpose,
never let it lapse by drifting. Re-author both fields every trial; they are
working memory, so rewrite them rather than append:

- `plan`: how you are spending the REMAINING trials — your budget across the
  run, where you stand against it, what currently limits progress (with the
  metric evidence), and your next move or two. This is where you actually decide
  the budget, so be concrete: name how many of the remaining trials you are
  giving to what, whether your current aim is met, and what you do next.
- `notes`: durable beliefs worth carrying — what works, what is a dead end, what
  looked like a fluke. Drop anything the latest evidence contradicts.
